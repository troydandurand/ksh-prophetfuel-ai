DEFENDANT’S MOTION FOR REASONABLE ACCESS TO COURT, LEGAL RESOURCES, AND DIGITAL CASE MATERIALS POST-FARETTA

[BOUNDS v. SMITH · LEWIS v. CASEY · TEX. CCP ART. 39.14 · § 504 REHABILITATION ACT · ADA TITLE II]

INTRODUCTION

Defendant Kyle Scott Harris, having elected self-representation pursuant to Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806 (1975), moves this Court for an order directing the Travis County Correctional Complex, 3614 Bill Price Rd, Del Valle, Texas 78617, to provide reasonable access to (1) a jail law library, (2) legal supplies and notary services, (3) a desktop or laptop computer with physical keyboard and mouse for research, discovery review, and motion drafting, and (4) jail-provided legal-assistance agents equipped with working accounts on Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and free legal-research tools. Without these accommodations, Defendant’s pro se defense is meaningful in name only, and the constitutional guarantees of meaningful access to the courts and effective self-representation are hollow.

STATEMENT OF FACTS

  1. Defendant is a United States Marine Corps [USMC] Gunnery Sergeant, Marine Special Operations Command [MARSOC], Scout Sniper, with awards including the Purple Heart and Silver Star, and is rated 100% disabled by the Department of Veterans Affairs [VA] with documented Traumatic Brain Injury [TBI] and Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder [PTSD] from combat operations.
  2. Defendant has been detained at the Travis County Correctional Complex since on or about November 22, 2025.
  3. Defendant has moved this Court to proceed pro se under Faretta v. California pursuant to the accompanying Motion to Proceed Pro Se.
  4. Discovery in this cause and the two related misdemeanor causes falls within Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Article 39.14 (Michael Morton Act) and is anticipated to include body-worn camera video files, cellular records in comma-separated-value [CSV] format, multi-hundred-page portable-document-format [PDF] exhibits, and audio recordings, all of which require desktop-class review software.
  5. Upon information and belief, the Travis County Correctional Complex provides tablet devices (Securus / JailATM or successor vendor) for inmate messaging, commissary, and limited education. Those tablets are not adequate for pro se discovery review or motion drafting for the reasons stated in the argument section below.
  6. Defendant is indigent and cannot afford to retain private research services, and the reasonable-access accommodations requested here are the least burdensome means by which this Court can secure the constitutional minimum guaranteed by Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817 (1977).

ARGUMENT

A. The constitutional floor — Bounds v. Smith.

The Supreme Court held in Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817, 828 (1977), that the fundamental constitutional right of access to the courts requires prison and jail authorities to assist inmates by providing either adequate law libraries or adequate assistance from persons trained in the law. The rule applies equally to pretrial detainees. Denial of both is denial of meaningful access.

The "actual injury" element of an access-to-courts claim was clarified in Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343, 350–351 (1996). Where a pro se defendant demonstrates that inadequate access has impeded a nonfrivolous defense or claim, actual injury is established. Every hour Defendant spends fighting an inadequate tablet interface to review discovery, draft motions, or research citations is an hour of prejudice.

B. Faretta right is meaningful only with meaningful tools.

Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806, 834–835 (1975), and its Texas analog under Texas Constitution Article I, Section 10, guarantee the right of the accused to represent himself. That right is a shell if the pro se defendant is denied the tools to exercise it. Federal courts have consistently held that Faretta imposes a corresponding duty on the detaining authority to provide reasonable access to research materials, drafting supplies, and legal reference systems.

C. Tablet interfaces are inadequate for pro se defense — specific technical grounds.

Defendant anticipates that the State or the detention authority may respond by pointing to tablet devices already provided at the Travis County Correctional Complex. That response fails on four specific technical grounds.

C.1 — Discovery review. Article 39.14 discovery arrives on data digital-versatile-discs [DVDs], universal-serial-bus [USB] drives, and cloud links containing body-worn camera Moving Picture Experts Group MPEG-4 [MP4] files, multi-hundred-page PDF exhibits, comma-separated-value [CSV] cell-tower logs, and audio recordings. Tablet operating systems cannot mount external drives natively, cannot scrub video timelines with mouse-precision, cannot open multi-window side-by-side comparison of exhibit against transcript, and cannot handle large discovery packages without cloud round-trips that a jail network will not permit.

C.2 — Legal research. Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and free tools (Cornell Legal Information Institute, Justia, Google Scholar, Fastcase, CourtListener) run either lite mobile versions or full desktop versions. Full desktop versions require a desktop browser. Tablet-lite versions lack citation copy-paste, advanced Boolean search, and multi-tab research workflow.

C.3 — Motion drafting. Long-form legal composition with mid-draft citation lookup requires a physical keyboard for speed and a physical mouse for precision. Touch drafting on tablet at length is measurably slower and produces measurably higher error rates. Under Lewis v. Casey, every measurable inefficiency is prejudice to the defense.

C.4 — iPad-plus-peripherals is still inadequate. Attaching a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse to an iPad or comparable tablet does not solve the underlying limitations of a touch-first operating system. iPadOS, Android, and comparable tablet operating systems (a) do not permit native mounting of external storage volumes containing discovery, (b) restrict application windowing to a limited split-view rather than true multi-window desktop workflow, (c) run stripped-down mobile versions of professional legal-research software, (d) impose file-management restrictions that block routine legal workflow such as bulk PDF annotation and mass exhibit organization, and (e) cannot run macro scripts or automation that competent legal drafting practice increasingly relies upon. A tablet with peripherals is not a desktop. The requested accommodation is a desktop or laptop computer proper.

D. Reasonable accommodation of documented disability.

Defendant is rated 100% disabled by the Department of Veterans Affairs [VA] with documented Traumatic Brain Injury [TBI] and Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder [PTSD] from Marine Special Operations Command [MARSOC] service. Fine-motor limitations and sustained-attention limitations make touch-first tablet interfaces materially less efficient than desktop input. A desktop or laptop computer with mouse and keyboard is a reasonable accommodation required by Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, 29 U.S.C. § 794, and by Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act [ADA], 42 U.S.C. § 12132. Jails are public entities within the meaning of both statutes. See Pennsylvania Dept. of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206 (1998).

E. The alternative Bounds prong — competent legal-assistance agents with real tools.

If the detention authority declines to provide direct computer access to Defendant under Prayer 3, the alternative Bounds prong of "adequate assistance from persons trained in the law" is triggered. Under that prong, the jail must supply legal-assistance agents (deputies, jail librarians, or contracted paralegal staff) who have working accounts and functional access to Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and free tools. An "assistance" program without research access is not assistance within the meaning of Bounds. The two prayers are alternatives on the Bounds spectrum but both are requested here so that if the Court grants only one, Defendant is not left below the constitutional floor.

F. Belt-and-suspenders record for § 1983 preservation.

Denial of both Prayer 3 and Prayer 4 leaves Defendant below the Bounds floor, satisfies the "actual injury" element of Lewis v. Casey, and preserves the record for a civil claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. Grant of any single prayer avoids that exposure for the detention authority.

G. Legal-mail privilege — incoming, outgoing, and digital.

All correspondence between Defendant and Defendant’s designated Durable Power of Attorney [DPOA] agent(s), and all correspondence between Defendant and the courts, the District Clerk, and any legal-research or drafting-support person acting at Defendant’s direction in preparation of Defendant’s pro se defense, is legal mail within the meaning of Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974), and Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78 (1987). Such mail may be opened for contraband inspection only in Defendant’s presence and may not be read, copied, delayed, disclosed, or catalogued by the State, the detention authority, or the prosecution.

The same privilege attaches to Defendant’s digital inbox and any electronic service address facilitated under Prayer 5 of this Motion. Digital legal mail is legal mail. The physical or electronic medium is immaterial to the privilege.

Any interception, opening, reading, delay, copying, disclosure, or non-delivery of Defendant’s legal mail or digital legal communications by any State actor, jail employee, prosecuting attorney, or agent thereof, shall be treated as (i) a violation of the Sixth Amendment right to self-representation under Faretta v. California, (ii) a violation of Article I, Section 10 of the Texas Constitution, (iii) potential tampering under Texas Penal Code § 37.10 (Tampering with Governmental Record), (iv) potential tampering under Texas Penal Code § 36.05 (Tampering with Witness), and (v) grounds for suppression, sanction, contempt, and preserved § 1983 exposure.

Defendant expressly reserves the right to file supplemental motions to suppress, to sanction, and to hold in contempt any State actor found to have breached the legal-mail privilege declared in this section.

PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Defendant Kyle Scott Harris, appearing pro se, respectfully prays that this Court enter an order directing the Travis County Correctional Complex, its Sheriff, and its designated agents, to provide the following access, effective immediately upon entry of this Court’s order granting Faretta self-representation:

  1. Prayer 1 — Jail Law Library Access. Reasonable and regular access to a physical jail law library or law-library equivalent containing at minimum the Texas Penal Code, Texas Code of Criminal Procedure, Texas Rules of Evidence, Texas Rules of Appellate Procedure, United States Code Titles 18, 28, and 42, the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, and West’s Southwestern Reporter or its digital equivalent, in a quiet space suitable for legal work, for not fewer than ten hours per week.
  2. Prayer 2 — Legal Supplies, Notary, and Postage. A reasonable and continuing supply of legal paper, pens, envelopes, and postage sufficient to prepare, sign, notarize, and file motions, correspondence with the Court, correspondence with prospective witnesses through counsel or licensed investigator, and correspondence with successor or standby counsel; free notary service on legal documents to be filed with any court; and Defendant’s outgoing legal mail postmarked no later than one business day after receipt from Defendant.
  3. Prayer 3 — Desktop or Laptop Computer With Physical Keyboard and Mouse.
    3(a) primary. Access to a desktop or laptop computer with physical keyboard and physical mouse, running a full desktop operating system, capable of (i) mounting external storage volumes to review discovery received under Article 39.14, (ii) opening and scrubbing body-worn camera video with mouse precision, (iii) opening multi-hundred-page PDF exhibits in multi-window comparison, (iv) running full desktop-browser sessions of Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, Cornell Legal Information Institute, Justia, Google Scholar, Fastcase, and CourtListener, and (v) drafting motions and correspondence in a word-processing application capable of standard legal formatting.
    3(b) first fallback. If a desktop is denied, a laptop with detachable physical keyboard and physical mouse.
    3(c) second fallback — record-preserving. If both are denied, an iPad or comparable tablet with attached Bluetooth keyboard and Bluetooth mouse, together with detailed jail-maintained logs of hours spent by Defendant on each defense task, which logs shall be produced to Defendant upon request for use in supporting a later "actual injury" showing under Lewis v. Casey.
  4. Prayer 4 — Jail Legal-Assistance Agents With Working Research Tools. Jail-provided legal-assistance agents (deputies, jail librarians, or contracted paralegal staff) supporting pro se defendants shall have working accounts and functional access to Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and the free tools listed in Prayer 3(a). Access shall include the ability to run keyword and Boolean queries on Defendant’s request, retrieve case law and statutory text within twenty-four hours of the request, and print relevant materials for delivery to Defendant at no cost to Defendant. This prayer is stated as an alternative on the Bounds spectrum to Prayer 3; both prayers are requested.
  5. Prayer 4A — DPOA Agent Research and Drafting-Support Access. The Court shall recognize that Defendant’s designated Durable Power of Attorney [DPOA] agent(s) may lawfully (i) hold professional accounts on Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and the free legal-research tools identified in Prayer 3(a), (ii) conduct case-law and statutory research at Defendant’s direction, (iii) prepare motion drafts, correspondence drafts, and research memoranda for Defendant’s review and signature, and (iv) deliver such drafts to Defendant through the legal-mail channel described in Section G of the Argument. This activity is preparatory research and drafting-support only; it does not constitute the practice of law, does not constitute legal representation, does not compromise Defendant’s pro se status under Faretta, and shall be treated as privileged defense work-product under the federal work-product doctrine and Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 192.5 (work-product privilege). Correspondence between Defendant and Defendant’s DPOA agent for the purpose of defense preparation is legal mail as declared in Section G below.
  6. Prayer 5 — Digital Inbox / Electronic Service Address. A jail-facilitated digital inbox or equivalent electronic service address permitting Defendant to receive electronically served motions, orders, and discovery from opposing counsel, the Court, and the Travis County District Clerk, and to transmit electronically signed filings to the Court and the District Clerk. Defendant’s existing detention communication account (Securus, JailATM, or successor) is not adequate for this purpose because it is not recognized by the Court’s e-filing system and is not accessible to opposing counsel for service. If no jail-facilitated digital inbox is available, Defendant requests an order permitting a designated agent under Durable Power of Attorney [DPOA] to serve as electronic service agent for receipt only, with the express reservation that such designation shall not constitute legal representation and shall not compromise Defendant’s pro se status.
  7. Prayer 6 — General Relief. Such further relief, at law or in equity, to which Defendant may show himself justly entitled.
