WHY THIS CARD EXISTS

Gunny — this card is for you whether you go pro se or keep counsel. If you go pro se, you file every one of these kites the same week. If you keep counsel, you still file them: better prepared, better organized, better client, better result. Attorneys who see a client this deep in the paperwork sharpen up. FORM 2 — COURTROOM ORAL REQUEST (post-Faretta hearing)

Stand, address the Court after Faretta is granted, deliver on the record.

Your Honor, having been granted the right to represent myself, I respectfully request that this Court enter an order directing the Travis County Correctional Complex to provide the following, which are prerequisites to meaningful self-representation under Bounds v. Smith, 430 U.S. 817 (1977).

First, reasonable access to a jail law library or law-library equivalent, minimum ten hours per week.

Second, legal supplies, notary services, and postage sufficient to prepare and file motions.

Third, access to a desktop or laptop computer with physical keyboard and mouse to review Article 39.14 discovery, conduct legal research on Westlaw, Lexis, Casetext, and free tools, draft motions, and save my work between sessions. A tablet, even with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, is not adequate for these purposes.

Fourth, a digital inbox or electronic service address recognized by the Court’s e-filing system.

Fifth, in the alternative or in addition, jail legal-assistance agents with working accounts on Westlaw, Lexis, and Casetext to support pro se defendants.

Sixth, in the alternative on the digital inbox request, leave to designate my Durable Power of Attorney [DPOA] agent as my electronic-service address for receipt-only, with the express reservation that this does not constitute legal representation and does not compromise my pro se status.

I am rated one hundred percent disabled by the Department of Veterans Affairs [VA] with documented Traumatic Brain Injury [TBI] and Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder [PTSD] from combat operations, which triggers Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act and Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act as additional grounds for these accommodations under Pennsylvania Department of Corrections v. Yeskey, 524 U.S. 206 (1998).

Denial of these requests would place me below the constitutional floor of Bounds v. Smith and satisfy the actual-injury element of Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996), preserving the record for further remedy including a claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

The written Motion covering these six requests is filed contemporaneously and is docketed as the Motion for Reasonable Access to Court, Legal Resources, and Digital Case Materials Post-Faretta.

FORM 4 — TACTICAL FRAMING (Kyle’s posture in every request)

  1. Ask in writing, always. Never verbal-only. Every denial creates a document.
  2. File the kite AND file the written Motion the same week: kite creates the administrative record, Motion creates the court record.
  3. Number every request and reference the number in follow-ups. Example: "Renewing Kite C dated ______, unanswered on day 8."
  4. Track jail response times. Kite C is Day 1. On Day 8 with no response, refile as Kite C-Renewed. On Day 15, file the § 1983 preservation notice.
  5. Frame every denial as a Lewis v. Casey injury log entry, dated and specific.
  6. Never argue with the deputy at intake. The deputy is not the decision-maker. The paper is.
  7. When the tablet is offered as a "solution," accept it under protest: "accepted with objection preserved under Bounds and Lewis v. Casey; iPad plus peripherals expressly stated inadequate on the record."
