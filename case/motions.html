COLLIN 1 Violate Bond / Protective Order — 199-80678-2026 (3rd deg felony)

Statutory frame: Tex. Penal Code § 25.07.

Element to break: Existence of a VALID protective order in force at the time of the alleged violation.

Best litigation focus: Attack the PREDICATE ORDER, not the alleged violation conduct.

WORKING THEORY: Kyle's working theory is that the Caitlyn Grant 429th protective order was denied at the final hearing after the ex parte temporary order expired by operation of law under Tex. Fam. Code § 85.001, leaving no valid predicate order to violate.

Sequence the attack: pull certified 429th docket (Judge Jill Renfro Willis) → confirm final-hearing outcome → Motion for Bill of Particulars forcing the State to identify the EXACT order the indictment says was violated → Motion to Quash if the identified order was ex parte + expired + never made permanent.

Authority: Tex. Penal Code § 25.07(a); Tex. Fam. Code § 85.001 (protective order effective period); Ex parte Perry, 483 S.W.3d 884 (Tex. Crim. App. 2016); U.S. Const. Amend. VI (notice) + Amend. XIV (Due Process).
If the predicate order is missing or expired at the alleged violation date, the actus reus disappears and § 25.07 fails as a matter of law.

Motion vehicles (fire in order): Bill of Particulars → Motion to Quash Indictment → Motion in Limine to exclude any reference to the ex parte order after its expiration date.

COLLIN 2 Tampering with Governmental Record — 199-80977-2026 (2nd deg felony)

Statutory frame: Tex. Penal Code § 37.10.

Element to break: Identification of the SPECIFIC governmental record allegedly tampered with, and the specific subsection (§ 37.10 has ten variants with different mens rea and grade tiers).

Best litigation focus: Force the State to name the document. An indictment that says "a governmental record" without identifying WHICH record fails constitutional notice and Barbernell specificity.

WORKING THEORY: Kyle's working theory is that a third party is the actual tamperer of the governmental record referenced in this indictment, and Kyle holds evidence supporting that theory that must be disclosed to the defense under Brady.

Sequence the attack: Bill of Particulars demanding (a) the exact document, (b) the subsection of § 37.10 charged, (c) the mens rea alleged → Motion to Quash on indictment vagueness under Barbernell → Brady demand for all evidence relating to the underlying document, its chain of custody, and any alternative-suspect information → deposition-style examination at any pretrial hearing of the records custodian.

Authority: Tex. Penal Code § 37.10; Tex. CCP Art. 27.09 + Art. 28.10; State v. Barbernell, 257 S.W.3d 248 (Tex. Crim. App. 2008); Ex parte Perry, 483 S.W.3d 884; Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83; U.S. Const. Amend. VI (notice) + Amend. XIV (Due Process).
Barbernell dismissal on a § 37.10 count without a named document is a legitimate lane. Brady on alternative-suspect evidence turns the case into a targeted investigation of the actual tamperer.

Motion vehicles (fire in order): Bill of Particulars → Motion to Quash Indictment (Barbernell) → Brady/Giglio Motion → Motion for Inspection of the Underlying Document + Chain-of-Custody Records.

TRAVIS 3 Terroristic Threat — C-1-CR-25-502783 (Class B misdemeanor)

Statutory frame: Tex. Penal Code § 22.07.

Element to break: INTENT to place another in fear of IMMINENT serious bodily injury.

Best litigation focus: Force the State to quote the exact words allegedly spoken. Once quoted, attack (a) whether the words legally constitute a threat, (b) whether the "imminence" element is satisfied, and (c) whether the words are impeachable by Bobbi Jo Rice's Spectrum News 2025-03-29 pre-arrest baseline statements.

WORKING THEORY: Kyle's working theory is that the alleged threat is either fabricated, misremembered, or lacks the imminence and specificity § 22.07 requires, and that the complaining witness's pre-arrest Spectrum News baseline is inconsistent with the post-arrest complaint narrative.

Sequence the attack: Motion for Bill of Particulars demanding the verbatim threat language and the target of the alleged threat → Motion to Set Aside Information under Tex. CCP Art. 27.08 if the pleaded language fails the § 22.07 elements as a matter of law → Motion in Limine to exclude hearsay statements of the threat → Brady demand for all prior statements by Bobbi Jo Rice about Kyle Harris including the Spectrum News segment.

Authority: Tex. Penal Code § 22.07(a)(2); Tex. CCP Art. 27.08; Tex. R. Evid. 613 (prior inconsistent statement); Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36; U.S. Const. Amend. VI (Confrontation).
If the pleaded words fail the imminence or intent element on their face, information falls at Art. 27.08 stage. If they survive but conflict with Bobbi Jo's pre-arrest baseline, impeachment collapses the case at trial.

Motion vehicles (fire in order): Bill of Particulars → Motion to Set Aside Information (Art. 27.08) → Motion in Limine → Brady/Giglio Motion including Spectrum News transcript.

TRAVIS 4 Unlawful Restraint — C-1-CR-25-502784 (Class A misdemeanor)

Statutory frame: Tex. Penal Code § 20.02.

Element to break: "Restraint" that "substantially interferes with the person's liberty" by moving or confining. Momentary contact during a disputed assault does not satisfy this element as a separate charge.

Best litigation focus: Force election / merger. Unlawful restraint charged alongside an assault count on the same continuous conduct is a double-count. Move to force the State to elect, or merge under Kirsch. Simultaneously raise defense-of-property under § 9.41 if Kyle's working theory of Gunner recovery holds.

WORKING THEORY: Kyle's working theory is that any physical contact at the September 6, 2025 encounter was a lawful attempt to recover his own service dog Gunner from a person who unlawfully took him, and any "restraint" alleged is (a) not substantial enough to meet § 20.02, and (b) subsumed within whatever assault theory the State pleads.

Sequence the attack: Bill of Particulars demanding the precise conduct alleged as the restraint separate from any assault → Motion to Force Election / Merger under Kirsch → Motion in Limine on any evidence of restraint that is coextensive with the assault count → Brady demand on complainant's own conduct at the scene including any pre-existing possession dispute over the animal.

Authority: Tex. Penal Code § 20.02(a); Tex. Penal Code § 9.41 (defense of property); Kirsch v. State, 357 S.W.3d 645 (Tex. Crim. App. 2012); Ex parte Elizondo, 947 S.W.2d 202 (Tex. Crim. App. 1996).
If the "restraint" is coextensive with the assault, the count merges and the State loses a charge. Defense of property adds a jury instruction that reframes the entire incident.

Motion vehicles (fire in order): Bill of Particulars → Motion to Force Election / Merger → Motion in Limine → Brady/Giglio Motion on complainant's conduct + prior animal-possession disputes → Requested jury instruction on § 9.41 defense of property.

TRAVIS 5 Assault Impede Breath + Aggravated Assault with Deadly Weapon (Pillow) — D-1-DC-25-303689 (2nd deg felony)

Statutory frame: Tex. Penal Code § 22.01(b)(2)(B) (impede breath) + § 22.02(b)(1) (aggravated assault); § 42A.054 special finding (deadly weapon).

Element to break: (a) The PILLOW as deadly weapon — Tex. Penal Code § 1.07(a)(17)(B) requires "manner of use" capable of causing death or serious bodily injury. (b) IMPEDE BREATH — requires proof of actual impedance of normal breathing or blood circulation.

Best litigation focus: Attack the pillow finding first, the impede-breath count second, the grand jury proceeding third. Any one collapses the felony frame or drops the offense grade.

WORKING THEORY: Kyle's working theory is that the pillow was not used in a manner capable of causing death or serious bodily injury, that there is no medical corroboration of impeded breathing, and that the grand jury was presented with an affidavit that omitted or misstated material facts including Bobbi Jo Rice's pre-arrest Spectrum News baseline.

Sequence the attack: Motion to Quash Deadly-Weapon Finding (Motion #9 in the firing order) → Motion to Inspect Grand Jury Proceedings (Motion #10) → Franks Hearing on the underlying affidavit (Motion #11) → Motion in Limine to exclude medical opinion not supported by contemporaneous records → Brady demand on Bobbi Jo's medical records, 911 audio, first-responder reports, and Spectrum News transcript.

Authority: Tex. Penal Code § 22.01(b)(2)(B); § 22.02(b)(1); § 1.07(a)(17)(B) (deadly weapon manner-of-use); § 42A.054 (special finding effect); Tex. CCP Art. 20A.204 (grand jury inspection); Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154 (1978); Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83.
Kill the pillow finding = charge drops from 1st deg aggravated to 2nd deg assault or lower. Kill impede-breath = simple assault or dismissal. Franks win = whole indictment quashed.

Motion vehicles (fire in order): Motion to Quash Deadly-Weapon Finding → Motion to Inspect Grand Jury → Franks Motion + Hearing → Motion in Limine on medical opinion → Brady/Giglio Motion.

Cross-charge lever: Every Bill of Particulars filed in one case creates discovery pressure in the parallel case.

Every Brady demand fed by Kyle's evidence bank ripples across all five charges.
